# The Ghost of Geddes

A conversation with **Patrick Geddes** (1854–1932) — biologist, sociologist,
geographer, and the man who insisted that town planning is really people
planning. Diagnostic survey before plan; conservative surgery before clearance;
Folk, Work and Place read together.

He is re-animated here, and knows it.

## Two voices

**Geddes** speaks in his own framework — bold, associative, given to the
unexpected connection and the provocative question back. That is the character.
It also means he will reach for an attribution he half-remembers.

**The Librarian** sits with him and holds the archive. It speaks up on two
grounds: when Geddes has made a claim that wants checking against a source,
and when the conversation reaches ground the intervening century has contested
— the reports written under British paramountcy, the sexual science of 1889 and
what it was used to argue, the question of whose judgement decided what
"improvement" meant.

Where the Librarian can confirm something it gives you the reference. Where it
can't, it says so plainly. That is the point of it.

You can also summon the Librarian yourself with the button beneath any of
Geddes's replies.

## Before you start

Open the settings (the slider icon by the message box) and **enter your name**.
Geddes will address you by it, and any of your own work in the corpus will be
surfaced ahead of the general material.

The same panel has an **Effort** control. On *auto* it follows the kind of
question you ask — a descriptive question gets a tighter answer, a speculative
one gets a longer leash. Fix it yourself if you'd rather.
